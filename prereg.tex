\section{What's the main question being asked or hypothesis being tested in this study?}

(RQ1) How is psychological distress expressed among temporary visa holders, and do patterns of expression differ
across visa category, gender, and household position? (RQ2) What conditions do participants themselves link to
distress, and do these attributions differ across visa category, gender, household position, and other features of
their circumstances?

\section{Dependent variable}

 (a) \emph{distress salience}; (b) \emph{condition salience}; (c) \emph{attribution} These will be joined at the person level to variables
already recorded in the study tracker: gender, visa status, age, country of origin, educational attainment, university,
major/field, employment status (org type/industry, company or institution name, job title), city/state, parent status,
couple number (links spouse pairs recruited together), interview date, interview length (minutes), and interviewer.

\section{Conditions}

Not applicable. Comparisons are across naturally occurring groups.

\section{Analyses}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), leftmargin=1.6em]
\item Primary: distress salience profiles by visa status, gender, and household position etc..
\item Secondary: latent class analysis on 6--9 distress indicators, with covariates drawn from the tracker (visa
status, gender, age, country of origin collapsed to region, educational attainment, employment status, household
position).
\item Attribution analysis: condition-by-distress co-occurrence heatmap, small multiples by visa status, elicited vs.\
volunteered reported separately.
\item Qualitative excerpts organized by condition.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Outliers and Exclusions}

Any visa status, country-of-origin/region, or other
demographic cell below 10 participants is collapsed per rules fixed before analysis.

\section{Sample Size}

$N = 195$ completed interview transcripts, fixed by already-completed
data collection; no further interviews will be conducted.
